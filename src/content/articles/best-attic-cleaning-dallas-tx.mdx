## What to Look For in a Dallas Attic Cleaning Company

Hiring an attic service company in Dallas requires attention to factors specific to North Texas conditions. First, ask about **R-value compliance for Texas Energy Code**. The current residential energy code recommends R-38 for attic insulation — roughly 10 to 14 inches of blown-in material depending on type. Any company quoting insulation work should specify the target R-value and the installed depth. Companies that don't discuss this are either unfamiliar with code requirements or planning to underfill.

Second, verify the company's experience with **storm damage assessment**. North Texas gets significant hail and wind events every spring, and roof damage often goes undetected until moisture problems appear in the attic weeks or months later. A good attic company should know what storm-related damage looks like — water stains on sheathing, displaced ridge vents, saturated insulation concentrated in specific areas — and should flag these issues even if you called them for a different reason.

Third, ask about **air sealing before insulation**. In Dallas's extreme heat, the gaps around plumbing penetrations, electrical boxes, can lights, and HVAC boots allow conditioned air to escape into the attic space and force your cooling system to work harder. Sealing these penetrations before new insulation goes in is the single highest-impact step in an attic energy upgrade. A company that skips it is leaving meaningful energy savings on the table. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should Dallas homeowners have their attic inspected?

At minimum, once per year — ideally after spring storm season has passed and before summer heat sets in. Spring storms in North Texas can damage roof components in ways that aren't visible from the ground, and catching moisture intrusion early prevents far more expensive mold remediation later. Homeowners in neighborhoods with mature trees should also inspect in late fall, as squirrels and rodents seek attic shelter as temperatures drop. If you notice a sudden spike in energy bills, uneven room temperatures, or visible light coming through the roof deck from inside the attic, schedule an inspection immediately.

### Is radiant barrier worth the investment in Dallas?

Yes — Dallas sits in the Department of Energy's hot-dry/mixed-humid climate zone where radiant barriers provide some of the highest returns in the country. A radiant barrier reflects solar radiation before it reaches the insulation layer, reducing peak attic temperatures by 20 to 30 degrees on summer afternoons. For a typical DFW home, that translates to 5 to 10 percent lower cooling costs during the six months of significant air conditioning use. Installation runs $700 to $1,500 and typically pays for itself within two to three cooling seasons. The benefit extends beyond energy savings — lower attic temperatures also slow insulation degradation, extend HVAC equipment life, and improve comfort in rooms directly below the attic.

### What makes Dallas attics different from other Texas markets?

Dallas attics face a unique combination of extreme summer heat, violent spring storms, and cold winter snaps that cities like Houston or Austin don't experience to the same degree. The metro regularly sees temperature swings of 40 to 50 degrees within a single week during transitional seasons, creating condensation conditions that more stable climates avoid. The prevalence of ranch-style homes with large, accessible attic footprints also means more surface area for heat gain and more linear footage of potential pest entry points along the roofline. These factors make proactive attic maintenance — regular inspection, timely insulation replacement, and air sealing — more important in Dallas than in markets with milder or more predictable weather patterns.
