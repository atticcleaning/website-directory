## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is mold so common in Houston attics?

Houston's humid subtropical climate keeps relative humidity above 60 percent for much of the year, which is the threshold where mold growth becomes possible on organic materials. Attics amplify this problem because they trap warm, moist air — especially when ventilation is inadequate or when bathroom exhaust fans vent directly into the attic space rather than to the exterior. The combination of Houston's ambient humidity, frequent heavy rain events, and the temperature differential between air-conditioned living space and superheated attic space creates persistent condensation on roof sheathing and rafters. That condensation provides the moisture mold needs to establish and spread, often long before homeowners notice any visible signs.

### Should I schedule attic work before or after hurricane season?

The ideal timing is before hurricane season, which runs from June through November. Scheduling insulation replacement, moisture barrier installation, or ventilation improvements in early spring gives your attic its best chance of surviving storm season intact. Properly installed insulation and sealed attic floors reduce the damage caused by wind-driven rain that enters through compromised roof areas. That said, if a storm has already damaged your attic, don't wait until the next off-season to address it. Saturated insulation and exposed wood sheathing will develop mold within 48 to 72 hours in Houston's humidity, so post-storm cleanup should happen as quickly as possible.

### How does Houston's humidity affect insulation lifespan?

Houston's humidity accelerates insulation degradation compared to drier climates. Blown-in fiberglass insulation absorbs ambient moisture over time, which increases its weight and causes it to settle and compress faster — reducing both its R-value and coverage area. Cellulose insulation is even more susceptible to moisture absorption, and in Houston's climate, it can clump and lose structural integrity after major humidity events or minor roof leaks. Where insulation in a dry climate might maintain adequate performance for 15 to 20 years, Houston attic insulation often needs assessment at the 8-to-12-year mark. Regular inspection is the best defense — catching moisture-damaged insulation early prevents the cascading problems of mold growth, pest attraction, and energy loss that follow.
